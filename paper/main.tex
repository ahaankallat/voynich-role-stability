\begin{abstract}
This paper studies internal role stability in the Voynich Manuscript without proposing decipherment, plaintext language, or translation. The analysis stops at the role layer. It asks whether written units in Voynichese keep stable internal roles after morphology aware grouping, multiple transcription support, controls for section or illustration code, Currier like class, hand like class, and line marker family, and sensitivity checks over tokenization assumptions. The evidence base is a multi transcription supported core of 1,709 lines and 7,107 token occurrences. The paper builds role test units from surface tokens, conservative morphology units, broad shape units, Stolfi inspired signatures, edge signatures, and boundary merge variants. It tests role stability with threshold sensitivity, ablation tests, random role baselines, held out validation, transcription tier checks, section level profiling, independent cluster induction, predictive held out validation, and tokenization robustness. The final reporting layer contains 23 globally reliable role units, 13 conditioned reliable role units, 9 environment sensitive reliable role units, and 9 tokenization robust reliable role units. The promoted reliable layer covers 1,903 of 7,107 token occurrences, or 26.8 percent of the analyzed core, and appears in 812 of 1,709 lines, or 47.5 percent of analyzed lines. A further good but not reliable enough layer covers 272 additional token occurrences. With this descriptive layer included, coverage reaches 2,175 token occurrences and 858 lines. These are internal distributional objects. They are not word meanings, parts of speech, formal grammar rules, or semantic claims.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords} Voynich Manuscript, Voynichese, undeciphered scripts, computational linguistics, morphology, role structure, transcription robustness, confound control, digital humanities.

\section{Introduction}

The Voynich Manuscript is an illustrated codex written in an undeciphered script and preserved as Beinecke MS 408 at Yale University Library \cite{yale_ms408}. Voynichese has long been known to have non random statistical structure. It also has major confounds. These include section, Currier variety, scribal hand, layout, line position, and uncertainty about what visible spaces mean \cite{currier_1976,bowern_lindemann_2021}. A role study must therefore avoid a simple error. It must not treat frequency, location, or word shape as proof of grammar.

This paper addresses that problem at the role layer. A role is not a meaning. A role is not a part of speech. A role is an internal distributional label assigned by earlier internal annotation work and then tested here for stability. The paper asks which written units behave as stable role units after the main known confounds are treated as controls.

The contribution is a maximal reliable catalog under the current data constraints. The word maximal does not mean complete. It means that the paper reports every unit that can be promoted under the stated rules, and also reports good but unpromoted units separately. A unit may be globally reliable, conditioned reliable, or environment sensitive reliable. The first class is portable across controls. The second class is reliable inside a stated manuscript environment. The third class is role stable and independently supported, but too concentrated in one environment to be called portable. This policy accepts that role behavior can be local without treating every local pattern as reliable.

The paper makes six contributions. First, it states the transcription and unit assumptions that support the analysis. Second, it uses prior Voynich word structure work as the morphology foundation for role test units. Third, it evaluates stability under sensitivity tests, ablations, random baselines, held out validation, transcription tier checks, and section profiles. Fourth, it adds independent cluster induction and held out role prediction as tests of whether the role layer can be recovered from morphology and context. Fifth, it separates reliable, good but not reliable enough, and low reliability material so that coverage can expand without weakening the main claim. Sixth, it tests whether promoted role units remain supported when visible spaces are not treated as the only possible unit boundary.

\section{Related work}

Currier identified large scale textual differences that later work treats as major structural varieties \cite{currier_1976}. IVTFF and related transcription work provide the multi witness setting in which different machine readable versions can be compared \cite{zandbergen_transcription}. Stolfi developed detailed models of Voynichese word structure, including prefix, midfix, suffix analysis and later word grammar work \cite{stolfi_pms_1997,stolfi_word_grammar_2000}. Computational studies have examined statistical structure, entropy, co occurrence patterns, and topic structure \cite{reddy_knight_2011,montemurro_zanette_2013,sterneck_topic_2021}. Bowern and Lindemann summarize the linguistic state of the field and emphasize that the manuscript remains undeciphered \cite{bowern_lindemann_2021}. Recent work also cautions that glyphs, tokens, and spaces should not be treated as ordinary letters, words, and spaces without argument \cite{parisel_units_2026}.

\section{Morphology foundation}

Role analysis should not begin with uninterpreted strings alone. Voynichese word forms have strong internal constraints, and prior work has modeled those constraints at the word grammar layer \cite{stolfi_word_grammar_2000}. The present paper therefore builds four role test lenses. These lenses are not claimed to be a complete morphology of Voynichese. They are operational ways to test whether role stability is visible at different unit granularities.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Role test lenses}
\label{tabmorph}
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{tables/morphology_lenses.tex}}
\end{table}

The conservative morphology lens groups tokens by limited frame material, body, terminal class, and reduced body shape. The broad shape lens groups reduced body shape and terminal class. The Stolfi inspired signature lens groups gallows pattern, reduced remainder shape, and terminal class. These lenses connect role testing to known word internal structure while avoiding a claim that the exact historical morphology is solved.

\section{Internal role layer}

The role labels tested in this paper were produced by earlier internal annotation work. That annotation work clustered token behavior by local context, line position, recurrence, and module structure. This paper does not ask the reader to accept those labels as semantic categories. It tests whether the labels behave as reproducible internal variables.

For each unit under each lens, the analysis computes total occurrences, distinct lines, distinct token types, dominant internal role, dominant role share, normalized role entropy, weighted within stratum role purity, agreement between local stratum majority role and global dominant role, number of combined strata, number of section or illustration codes, metadata concentration values, and maximum metadata concentration risk.

A unit is called role stable across confounds under the standard threshold only when it has enough frequency, enough line support, high dominant role share, high within stratum role purity, high stratum majority agreement, adequate spread across strata, adequate spread across sections, and low maximum metadata concentration risk. For morphology based lenses, at least two surface token types are required. This prevents a family result from merely restating one surface token.

\section{Evaluation design}

The evaluation is designed around skeptical alternatives. A stable unit could be a frequency artifact. It could be a line position artifact. It could be section, Currier, hand, morphology, or threshold choice. Threshold sensitivity means that the analysis is rerun under strict, standard, and loose promotion cutoffs. If a unit appears only at one cutoff, it is treated with more caution.

The paper uses eight tests. First, threshold sensitivity compares strict, standard, and loose criteria. Second, ablation tests show how many candidates survive when controls are added. Third, random role shuffle baselines test whether stable units appear after role labels are destroyed globally or within control strata. Fourth, held out validation defines units on development data and tests whether the same dominant role appears in held out folios or lines. Fifth, transcription tier robustness checks whether the full supported units are seen and role consistent in the S1 and S2 evidence tiers. Sixth, independent cluster induction groups units from morphology and context features without using role labels as input features. Seventh, predictive validation asks how well held out role labels can be recovered from morphology and context features without using manuscript section, Currier class, hand class, or line marker family as input features. Eighth, tokenization robustness tests whether the same role support appears under surface token, morphology, shape, edge, and boundary merge variants.

These tests do not prove grammar or meaning. They ask whether the role layer is strong enough to be a reproducible object for later grammar work.

\section{Environment sensitive expansion}

The environment sensitive expansion responds to a stricter version of the role question. Global portability is sufficient evidence, but it is not necessary evidence. Some role units may be real while being concentrated in one textual environment. It therefore adds environment sensitive reliable units when three conditions hold. The unit must have high role purity, enough occurrence and line support, and independent induction support from morphology and context features. \section{Tokenization robustness and final coverage}

Visible space tokenization is a defensible starting point, but it is not treated as final. The tokenization robustness stage tests whether candidate role units remain supported when the unit boundary is varied. The tested variants include surface tokens, conservative morphology units, broad shape units, Stolfi inspired signatures, frame and terminal units, token edge signatures, and left or right boundary merge signatures. The boundary merge checks ask whether a role signal depends on treating the visible space on either side of the unit as fixed.

A tokenization robust unit is promoted only if it keeps high role purity, has independent induction support, and has tokenization support from at least two unitization variants. This rule lets coverage rise, but it blocks candidates that look good only under one segmentation assumption.

\section{Limitations}

The main limitation is that the role labels come from an upstream internal annotation layer. Independent clustering and held out prediction reduce this dependence, but they do not fully replace the upstream layer with a new raw manuscript role induction system.

A second limitation is that the morphology layer is operational. It is based on known word structure ideas, but it is not a complete historical morphology. A third limitation is that conditioned reliable units and environment sensitive reliable units are not globally portable and should not be cited as global role evidence. A fourth limitation is that transcription tier robustness is not the same as a full leave one witness out test, because the available project table records support tiers rather than all individual witness strings. A fifth limitation is that held out tests are smaller than the full corpus and therefore conservative frequency thresholds can remove otherwise role consistent units.

These limitations define the claim. The paper establishes a role stability layer that can be independently rerun and challenged. It does not establish formal grammar or semantics.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper provides a morphology aware and multi transcription controlled account of internal role stability in Voynichese. The strongest result is a reliable catalog of role units that survives combined controls, threshold checks, ablations, random role baselines, held out validation, transcription tier checks, section profiling, independent cluster induction, held out predictive validation, and tokenization robustness checks. The tokenization robustness stage expands coverage by promoting only the additional units that survive alternate unit boundary checks.

The result is intentionally limited. It is not a decipherment. It does not identify a language, a lexicon, parts of speech, phonetic values, formal grammar, or semantics. Its contribution is the reproducible role layer that must come before those stronger claims.
